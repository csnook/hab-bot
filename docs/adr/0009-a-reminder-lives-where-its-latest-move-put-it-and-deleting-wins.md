# A reminder lives where its latest move put it, and deleting wins over what other devices did meanwhile

Each reminder list is its own stream (ADR 0005), so moving a reminder is a change to two streams that devices out of touch can make at once. Moving writes one event, `ReminderMovedIn`, in the destination's stream, carrying a hybrid logical clock. A reminder is in the list whose stream says it arrived last (a reminder made in a list arrived there at the earliest clock), and every device that holds the account's lists works out the same answer. The reminder keeps its id, and so do its occurrences and their ids. The new list takes in everything every other list has of the reminder, as a union: its settings with every value they ever had, its occurrences, closings, snoozes, alerts and notices. So an action another device made in the old list after the move was made is not lost, and the order in which devices' events reach the server doesn't matter. Deleting a reminder and keeping its history is a tombstone event (`ReminderDeleted`): it stops the reminder firing, alerting and being listed on every device that has the event, and nothing else brings it back. Deleting it with its history is `ReminderPurged`: devices that have the event forget the reminder, in every list, and delete the events they stored of it.

## Considered Options

- **Copy the reminder's events into the destination, and a move-out event in the source**: two events that can be separated by a crash or by two devices' uploads, leaving the reminder in both lists or in neither, and copied events would have to be signed again, losing who and when.
- **A snapshot of the reminder in the destination's move event**: each stream is then readable alone, which sharing a list will need (the people it is shared with must not need the old list's key, ADR 0002). Not done yet: every list of the account is held by every device of the account, so the old list is always at hand. The move to or from a shared list will need this.
- **An edit to the reminder in the old list wins over a deletion if it is later**: a deleted reminder could come back from a stale device, firing and alerting again, which is the worse mistake. A deletion is therefore final.
- **Purge by rewriting the stream**: the server can't tell which of its encrypted events belong to a reminder, so it can't remove them. Not attempted.

## Consequences

- Of two moves of one reminder made out of touch, the later clock wins on every device, so the user who moved it first sees it move again once they sync.
- A reminder that was deleted keeps its history marked deleted, with its settings as they were. An edit, move or firing made meanwhile on another device is kept in the history and has no effect. An occurrence it had open stays in the history, open but not listed, never alerting, and can't be acted on.
- A deletion made while another device fires the reminder doesn't reach that device's alert in time: it alerts until it hears. Devices on an older format don't read the event at all and go on alerting; they show "Update the app to see recent changes to this list".
- A purge reaches every device that syncs, including events made by devices that hadn't heard of it, which are dropped as they arrive. It can't recall copies on devices that never sync again, and the server keeps the encrypted events until retention limits and snapshots (sync spec) can remove them. The first version says "delete with its history" for what the user sees; the server's copy is gone only by that later work.
- A list is deleted only while it holds no reminders: `ListDeleted` hides it, and a reminder another device made or moved into it meanwhile keeps it. Its stream stays on the server for the same reason as above. The personal list can't be deleted or renamed.
- A list is made on one device with a fresh id and its first key is derived from the personal key like the personal list's (ADR 0008). After a device has been removed that key could be worked out by the removed device, so a list made after that also gets a random second version, sealed to the devices that remain, and its events use that.
- The sidebar's filters are kept on the device and applied by the window. The alerter never reads them, so hiding a list or a priority hides it from the window only.
- New events are format 8 (`Event::format`). An older app keeps them without applying them.
