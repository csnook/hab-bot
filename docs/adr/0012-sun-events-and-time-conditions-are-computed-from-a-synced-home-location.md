# Sun events and time-based conditions are worked out on each device from a synced home location

Sun events and the daylight and darkness conditions need a place, and places come after the first release. So the first release has one: the user's **home location**, their Home place (a centre and the default 150 m radius, in degrees), set by the user. Every device evaluates a reminder from the same data, so evaluation is a pure function and two devices agree without talking to each other.

## Decisions

- **The home location is a personal setting that syncs.** It is `Event::HomeSet { hlc, place }` in the user's personal list, `None` clearing it, and of several the latest hybrid logical clock wins on every device (as `ListPaused` does, ADR 0011). Only the personal list's counts. It is not a device setting: the user has one home, and a desktop that never moves still needs it to evaluate their reminders. `Core::set_home`, `clear_home` and `home` are its API; Settings → You edits it as a latitude and longitude, and the reminder editor asks for it the first time a reminder uses a sun event or a daylight condition and none is set. "Use where I am now" is for phones, which have no app yet.
- **A sun event is `{ event, offset_minutes }`**, event one of sunrise, sunset, civil dawn and civil dusk, the offset up to 12 hours either way. They are instants predicted like a schedule's: `Core::instances` is the one source of a repeating reminder's instants, from its schedules and its sun events, so firing, `next_fire_at`, expected occurrences, pausing, expiry and overdue all work on them unchanged. The occurrence's id is the reminder plus the instant, so two devices' firings merge.
- **The calculation is NOAA's solar calculator (Meeus), in `sun.rs`, with no network.** Sunrise and sunset are the Sun's centre 0.833° below the horizon, civil twilight 6°. Tests check it against PyEphem for London, New York, Sydney, Quito, Kiritimati and Tromsø, daylight-saving days and polar day and night, to within two minutes (three near the polar circles). A day is the solar day at the home location, so a sunrise can fall on the previous UTC date. A day on which the event doesn't happen has no instant, so nothing fires. On the days the Sun only grazes the horizon the answer can differ from an almanac's by a day.
- **A time-based condition is days of the week, a time window (crossing midnight when it ends earlier), a date range, a season of the year (wrapping the new year), daylight or darkness**, combined with AND. `Condition::holds(instant, zone, home)` reads the instant in the reminder's zone (its pinned zone, or else the device's), so a 07:00 reminder with a 06:30 to 07:30 window passes on both sides of a clock change. Days are the calendar day of the trigger. Daylight is the Sun's centre above -0.833° at the instant, worked out directly, so polar day and night need no special case.
- **They never make a reminder wait.** An instant outside them is absent from `instances`: no occurrence, no waiting, no pause skip recorded, no tentative expected occurrence, and an open occurrence stays open (only firing again expires it, ADR 0001). The Waiting group of the tray and the Inbox stays empty until conditions that can't be predicted exist.
- **Conditions apply to schedules and sun events,** not to one-offs or countdowns, which the core refuses them on. A countdown "always waits" on a condition (spec), and a one-off outside its condition would never fire; both need the waiting state this release doesn't build.
- **A condition that can't be checked counts as met:** daylight and darkness with no home location. A sun event with no home has no instant, so it doesn't fire, and the reminder's view says `needs_home`. The spec's note on the occurrence ("couldn't check") is not recorded yet.
- **Editing is not retroactive.** A change to a reminder's schedules, zone, sun events or conditions moves `active_from`, and sun events fire only for instants after the home location was set, so loosening a condition or setting a home doesn't fire what already passed.
- **New events are format 11** (`HomeSet`, the sun-event and condition settings, and a creation event that carries either). An older app keeps them without applying them. A reminder made with sun events or conditions is therefore unknown to it from the start and never alerts there. But conditions added later to a reminder the older app already knew are not applied by it: it **keeps firing that reminder on every day** until it is updated.

## Considered options

- **A device setting for the home location:** a second desktop would then need setting up by hand, and the later place features build on a synced Home place.
- **Waiting for time-based conditions:** the spec says they pass, because a weekday-only reminder should show nothing on Saturday.
- **A network sun API:** rejected: the app works offline and standalone.
